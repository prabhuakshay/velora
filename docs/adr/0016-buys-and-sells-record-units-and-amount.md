# Buys and Sells record units and Amount; Trade Price is derived

A Buy or Sell records its units and its Amount, the trade value before charges, and nothing else about its price. Its Trade Price is worked out as Amount ÷ units whenever it is shown, so it can never disagree with the money the books use: cost, Lots, the ledger and replay all read the Amount. Units and Amount are the two figures a mutual fund statement, CAS or contract note gives exactly; NAVs are published to 4 decimals and units rounded to 3, so the per-unit price rarely multiplies out to the Amount to the paisa. Entering all three and cross-checking them was set aside because the check needs a tolerance anyway and still leaves a third number that feeds nothing. Entering units and Price and deriving the Amount was set aside because the derived Amount would differ from the statement by rounding, and the Amount is what the ledger must match. The Investment Import's price column stays for readability: it is optional, and when given it must agree with units × price within ₹1 or 0.1% of the Amount, whichever is larger, which catches typos. Before the stored price was dropped, existing events whose price disagreed by more than that were listed for the user to fix.
