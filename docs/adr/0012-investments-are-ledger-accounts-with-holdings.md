# Investments are ledger Accounts with Holdings kept beside them

An Investment Account is an ordinary Asset Account for one broker or fund platform, so money moving between it and a bank is a Transaction like any other. The units, Lots and Prices of what it holds live in a separate holdings layer, linked to the Transaction that paid for a Buy when there is one. Net Worth counts the Investment Account at its Market Value, not its Balance. Two other designs were set aside. Investments as plain Asset Accounts only (one per fund) know the money paid but not the units, so Market Value, returns and XIRR can't be worked out, and Net Worth ignores the market. A portfolio tracker outside the ledger knows the units, but the bank side of a Buy then has to go somewhere: recording it as spending blows Budgets and counts every Sell as income, and leaving it out stops bank Balances matching the bank. Keeping both means two records of one Buy, held together by the link and by the Investment Account's Cash. Sells use FIFO only, as Indian mutual funds and demat accounts do, so the user never picks which Lots a Sell takes.
